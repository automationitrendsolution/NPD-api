1Overview

The Amazon Research tool lets you search Amazon product listings and scrape detailed product data and customer reviews directly from the NPD Pipeline UI. It powers the competitor analysis that feeds the Tier 1 scoring pipeline — specifically the Saturation, Differentiation, and Economic Viability sub-scorers.

What it does: Sends a real search query to Amazon, parses the SERP (Search Engine Results Page), and returns up to 20 product cards per page. You can then drill into any ASIN for full product detail.
SERP Scraping
Up to 20 products per page, multiple pages supported
Product Detail
Price, BSR, ratings, reviews, bullet points, images
Review Scraping
Customer reviews per ASIN for sentiment analysis

2How to Search

Navigate to Tools → Amazon Research in the sidebar, or use the Quick Action on the Dashboard.

1
Enter a keyword Type the exact search term you want to research (e.g. "bamboo travel mug"). Use the same keyword you plan to score in Tier 1 for the most relevant competitor data.
2
Choose a page Page 1 shows the top ~20 organic results — the most relevant competitors. Page 2+ shows lower-ranked products. For Tier 1 analysis, always start with page 1.
3
Click Search Results load within 3–8 seconds. Each scraping request costs 1 credit from your ScrapingBee quota.
4
Drill into a product Click any product card to fetch full detail (price history, BSR, all bullet points, image count). This costs 1 additional credit.
Each search and each product detail request uses 1 ScrapingBee credit. A typical research session (1 SERP + 3 products) costs 4 credits. Monitor your quota in the ScrapingBee dashboard.

3Understanding Search Results

Each product card on the SERP contains the following parsed fields:

FieldDescriptionUse in Scoring
asinAmazon Standard Identification Number — unique product IDUsed to fetch product detail pages
titleFull product listing title as shown on AmazonDifferentiation scorer checks title uniqueness
priceCurrent listed price (USD)Economics scorer — must be ≥ $15 to pass price floor
ratingStar rating (1.0–5.0)Saturation scorer — high ratings = strong competition
review_countNumber of customer reviewsSaturation scorer — >500 reviews = high barrier to entry
bsrBest Seller Rank in primary categoryDemand scorer — low BSR = high sales velocity
is_amazon_choiceWhether the product has Amazon's Choice badgeDifferentiation signal
is_sponsoredWhether the listing is a paid adIndicates competitive PPC market
image_urlPrimary product image URLDisplay only
Tip: The first 3–5 organic (non-sponsored) results are the most important. A SERP where all top spots are dominated by brands with 1,000+ reviews and 4.5+ ratings indicates a high-saturation market — expect a lower saturation score.

5Review Scraping (Tier 2A)

Once an idea has been shortlisted (manually promoted on the Task Board), you can trigger full customer review scraping via the API or the Idea Detail page. This is Tier 2A of the pipeline.

What it scrapes

  • Up to 3 competitor ASINs (configurable, max 5)
  • Up to 3 pages of reviews per ASIN (configurable, max 10)
  • Each review: title, body, rating, date, verified purchase, helpful votes

Triggering a scrape

Via the API (POST to /api/reviews/scrape/):

POST /api/reviews/scrape/ { "idea_id": "uuid-...", "max_asins": 3, "max_pages": 3, "force": false }
The idea must have status = "shortlisted" and must have completed Tier 1 scoring (so competitor ASINs are available in tier1_evidence). The scrape costs up to 9 credits (3 ASINs × 3 pages).

Where reviews are stored

Reviews are stored in the idea document under reviews_data and the flag reviews_scraped: true is set. The LLM Tier 2C analysis reads these reviews to generate its synthesis.

6Pipeline Integration

The Amazon Research tool is used at two distinct points in the pipeline:

Tier 1 — Auto (during scoring)

When you click Run Tier 1 Scoring, the scorer automatically fetches the SERP and top product pages. You do not need to run a manual research first.

Tier 2A — Manual (after shortlisting)

After an idea is promoted to Shortlisted on the Task Board, you manually trigger review scraping from the Idea Detail page or via the API.

Data flow diagram

Keyword input ↓ Amazon SERP scrape → top 20 products, prices, ratings, BSRs ↓ Product detail scrape → bullet points, category, monthly_sales_est ↓ Tier 1 sub-scorers → demand / saturation / differentiation / economics ↓ Idea shortlisted by human ↓ Review scraping (2A) → 3 ASINs × 3 pages of customer reviews ↓ LLM analysis (2C) → GPT-4o synthesis of all evidence ↓ Pre-sourcing doc (3) → product spec, listing draft, sourcing brief

7Tips & Limits

Best practices

  • Research the exact keyword you plan to score — Amazon's SERP varies significantly between "bamboo mug" and "bamboo travel mug".
  • Page 1 is the gold standard. If page 1 is dominated by brand-name products with thousands of reviews, it is a high-saturation niche.
  • Sponsored products (ads) at the top of page 1 are normal — scroll down to see the true organic competition.
  • A low BSR (e.g. <5,000 in a subcategory) means the product is selling very well — strong market demand signal.
